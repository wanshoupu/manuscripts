    \begin{abstract}
        Quantum software development is challenged by hardware heterogeneity and the lack of unified compilation frameworks.
        We present a versatile quantum compiler designed to decompose arbitrary Hamiltonians into quantum circuits expressed via a platform-independent intermediate language (IL).
        This IL serves as a universal representation, enabling flexible translation and optimization across diverse quantum hardware backends.
        We also present designs of renderers to convert the IL into executable quantum circuits into the target quantum computing platforms such as Qiskit and Cirq.
    \end{abstract}

    \section{Relevance}\label{sec:relevance}
    This poster proposal is in the domain of Quantum Systems Software to bridge the gap between physics modeling and quantum software development.
    It demonstrated an implementation of quantum circuit synthesizer trackable lineage.

    Quantum software development is currently hindered by fragmentation across diverse hardware platforms and proprietary compiler toolchains.
    Each major quantum software development kit (SDK)—such as Qiskit, Cirq, or AWS Braket—has its own interface and backend assumptions,
    forcing developers to rewrite or reoptimize code for each platform~\cite{qiskit2023,cirq2024,braket-zenodo}.
    This siloed ecosystem complicates benchmarking, hinders reproducibility, and slows innovation.

    To address these limitations, we introduce a cross-platform quantum compiler that accepts user-defined Hamiltonians as input and
    produces optimized executable quantum circuits across multiple quantum platforms.
    By decoupling high-level algorithm design from hardware-specific details, our approach promotes abstraction, portability, and reproducibility.

    Our qompiler translates Hamiltonians expressed as linear combinations of Pauli strings using a multi-stage decomposition framework.
    This includes two-level decomposition (2L), swap gate decomposition (CX), control decomposition (CTRL), Euler angle decomposition (EA),
    and the Solovay-Kitaev approximation (SK)~\cite{nielsen2010quantum}.
    The tool exposes configurable options for decomposition granularity and optimization tiers, enabling users to tailor circuit generation
    to the native constraints of specific hardware.

    Initial benchmarks confirm the correctness and consistency of the output across supported platforms, including Qiskit and Cirq.
    We are actively extending compatibility to additional frameworks such as AWS Braket and PennyLane, with the broader goal of streamlining
    quantum algorithm development across the heterogeneous quantum landscape~\cite{braket-zenodo,pennylane}.
