    \begin{abstract}
        We present a quantum compiler capable of decomposing arbitrary Hamiltonians into quantum circuits in terms of a platform-independent intermediary language (IL).
        We also present renderers to render the IL into executable quantum circuits into the target quantum computing platforms.
        Our compilation methodology includes multi-stage decompositions such as two-level decomposition (2L), Swap decomposition (CX), control decomposition (CTRL),
        Euler decomposition (ED), and/or Solovay-Kitaev approximation (SK).
        The decomposition granularity is configurable, chosen to optimized for different types of quantum computing hardware.
        The tool currently supports Qiskit and Cirq and we have plans to extend to other popular platforms of today.
    \end{abstract}

    \section{Introduction}
    Quantum software development faces fragmentation due to heterogeneous hardware and compiler stacks.
    Developers often write bespoke code for each quantum SDK (e.g., Qiskit, Cirq, Braket).
    To bridge this gap, we propose a cross-platform quantum compiler that inputs a user-defined Hamiltonian and outputs an optimized circuit executable on multiple quantum platforms\cite{b1}.


    \section{Motivation}
    The lack of standardized workflows hinders reproducibility and slows research progress.
    Current toolchains lock users into specific ecosystems.
    Our compiler promotes abstraction, portability, and rapid prototyping.


    \section{Method}
    Given an input Hamiltonian $H = \sum_j c_j P_j$ expressed as a linear combination of Pauli strings, we decompose $H$ using operator synthesis techniques and compile it into gate-level circuits. The compiler backend maps circuits into native gates for Qiskit, Cirq, or other selected platforms. Users can toggle basis sets and optimization levels.

    \subsection{Gate Translation Pipeline}
    Each Pauli term is individually mapped via exponentiation: $e^{-iHt} \approx \prod_j e^{-ic_j P_j t}$. Our pipeline then:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Translates Pauli exponentials into native gate sequences.
        \item Applies platform-specific transpilation.
        \item Outputs backend-executable code with minimal overhead.
    \end{enumerate}

    \section{Results}
    Initial benchmarks show the compiler produces correct circuits consistent across backends.
    Output fidelity has been validated on local simulators.
    Early adopters noted improvements in modularity and experimentation speed.


    \section{Conclusion and Future Work}
    This project reduces platform friction in quantum software development.
    Future work includes:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Integrating PennyLane and AWS Braket support
        \item Adding classical-quantum hybrid workflows
        \item Implementing circuit equivalence checking
    \end{itemize}
