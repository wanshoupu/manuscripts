\section{Variational method for optimal design and implementation}

How I came to a better design by introducing a method called isEmpty()

When handling the visitation of repeated fields of protobuf during a design of visitor pattern,
my brained was twisted several rounds.
A repeated field is a field where a list of elements may be recorded in one place. In general,
The significances of repeated fields are the size of the list, the positional indexes, and the
elements themselves. On the one hand, the repeated field itself may be of interest, even elements
are empty messages. On the other the elements themselves are often more important. But under
certain circumstances, not all of the info is desirable. For example when counting for the
presence of fields, we want just a Boolean per field. More info is simply a burden. But my
visitor pattern is designed for general use.
I tried with more complex design of the element classes. I also tried with more complex algorithm
how to avoid recording empty messages. All go no avail.
Finally, I approached the problem from another perspective. Treat head for headache and treat
foot for footache. Because the problem
is with the specific use case of field presence tally, I put a special logic to count the
presence of the total elements in the repeated field. Then I put a recursive algorithm to remove
the ancestors of nodes where count = 0. It worked! For this specific case!
Then I abstracted the concept to represent the count = 0 condition as isEmpty and put this in the
base class. Now my visitor pattern design and implementation is more flexible and general
purposed. It allowed user to specify if repeated field significance is of importance while shift
away the task of removing the unimportant parts from the overall result. That is by tuning the isEmpty method.
A good design takes away much of the weight lifting things that’s common and known while leaving
the variable parts and use case specifics to client to make a call. The balance may be subtle and
the solution may be scarce, but there seems always a good design somewhere. It clicks with your
brain when you found it.

I have to admit by now that I’m no genius. I’m quite clumsy, witless, and ordinary. My way of
working is not genius like at all. Nevertheless I can produce something of quality with hard
work, long-time thinking, perseverance, and Godly inspiration, and a good sense of judgement.
Most importantly, I used variational methods to approach the desirable result. Hard work. With
any idea of any quality, you need to put it to experiment. Start with an implementation and see
its result. No idea should go untested. That requires hard work. Long-time thinking. You cannot
work for long without thinking. Take a walk away from your desk to help you concentrate. More
often than not, ideas will be like rain pour over you while you are walking after a long
struggling work at no avail. Perseverance. You can be passionate but that state won’t last
without perseverance. Many times i observed myself become impatient, desperate, depressed in that
order. But I kept going on anyway. At various stages, I was almost convinced that there is no way
out or I cannot find a way out. But due to a trace of belief and perseverance, I didn’t give up.
For example, I am not happy with a design of template interface at some point. My gut feeling
tells me that there got to be a better way to arrange the types, algorithms, interfaces such that
the template has minimum implementation required. I believe it’s a suboptimal design. But I’ve
been trying for several times with no hope of seeing this process converging anywhere. Godly
inspiration. I prayed, a lot. I take breaks by prayers and Bible study. It’s not for profit that
I turn to our Lord Jesus. I just enjoy being around of Him. His presence comforts me. God is my
Shepherd I shall not be in want. I depend on Gods inspiration. And He never let me down. When I
walk or on train, ideas pour on me like rain drops. I have to hurry to write them down before
they dry out on my! A sense of good judgement. This tells me when to keep working, keep
searching, keep experimenting. This also tells me when to pause and accept what I got. Quality of
work does not come into being all of a sudden. It’s a process of improvement. It’s like the
learning curve. It’s nonlinear. Sometimes the quality of work doesn’t improve for a long time.
Some times it regresses. On rare occasions, it improves with a good idea by a great amount. Good
judgement is like eyes guides you through the paths of a maze. Good work is like a ‘click’. You
can sense the imperfect. You can feel the problems underlying because you have the discomfort
right there. Your gut feeling even tells you how big the problem is.
Lastly let’s talk about the method-variational method. It’s bit by bit. It’s convergence oriented
. It’s detail go last. Variational method requires a goal in mind in the first place. Then
approach the goal from a starting point. There are generally two phases. In phase one, You
experiment with various approaches. It’s explorational. Don’t try to pin on too much details.
After evaluation of the approaches, choose one and plug through it. If you can reach the goal,
that means it’s a viable approaches. Second phase is converge bit by bit. You vary some steps and
by good judgement, you can feel the improvement or regression. Also you nitpick on some flaws.
You keep improve. You try various ways to get rid of the flaw. Don’t let go if it’s still there.
The wonderful thing is that as you strive to get rid of the uneasiness in you, you often can find
out much much better ways to do it. And this proves your gut feeling is real!
