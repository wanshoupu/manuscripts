    \begin{abstract}
        We present a quantum system software framework that bridges the gap between physics modeling and quantum software development.
        At the core of this framework is a versatile quantum compiler capable of decomposing arbitrary Hamiltonians into quantum circuits, represented using a platform-independent B-Tree intermediate language (IL).
        The B-Tree structure provides essential lineage information for circuit gate traceability and code verification.
        The IL offers a universal, hardware-agnostic representation that enables flexible translation and optimization across diverse quantum hardware backends.
        We demonstrate implementations that render the IL into executable quantum circuits targeting platforms such as Qiskit and Cirq.
        Additionally, we provide a transpiler that converts the IL into OpenQASM for broader compatibility.
    \end{abstract}

    \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

    Quantum simulation offers exponential advantages for applications in chemistry, pharmaceuticals, and biological processes.
    Despite its potential, developing quantum software remains highly challenging, demanding deep expertise in physics, mathematics, and software engineering.
    This steep learning curve limits access for many users and professionals who could potentially benefit.
    Additionally, existing quantum transpilers are often platform-specific, done in ad hoc, and lack flexible control over compilation granularity.

    To overcome these challenges, we developed \texttt{qompiler}, a cross-platform quantum circuit synthesizer designed for traceability, verifiability, and configurable gate control.
    It decomposes Hamiltonians into circuits using a multi-stage framework including two-level, swap, control, Euler angle decompositions, and Solovay-Kitaev approximations.
    The intermediate representation (IL) is structured as a B-Tree, enabling gate-level lineage and is platform-independent, supporting major frameworks
    such as Qiskit, Cirq, and OpenQASM\@.
    Importantly, the compiler allows fine-grained gate selection to match native hardware capabilities—for example,
    using Pauli gates on superconducting hardware or rotation gates on trapped-ion devices—facilitating hardware-aware optimization and broad applicability.


    \section{Method}\label{sec:method}
    The proposed compiler, \texttt{qompiler}, decomposes user-defined Hamiltonians into executable quantum circuits
    through a multi-stage decomposition framework.
    This framework integrates several key techniques, including two-level decomposition (2L), swap gate decomposition (CX),
    control gate decomposition (CTRL), Euler rotation decomposition (EA), and the Solovay-Kitaev approximation (SK)~\cite{nielsen2010quantum}.
    The decomposition process is highly configurable, offering users fine-grained control over decomposition granularity
    and optimization levels to suit the native constraints of targeted quantum hardware.
    The intermediate and final circuit representations are captured using a B-Tree data structure,
    which encodes gate-level lineage via parent-child relationships among tree nodes, enabling robust traceability
    and verifiability throughout the compilation process.

    The compiler’s intermediate language (IL) is platform-independent and can be rendered into executable circuits for specific quantum platforms
    as well as platform-independent standards such as OpenQASM for broader compatibility~\cite{openqasm2,openqasm3}.
    A key feature of \texttt{qompiler} is its configurable compilation granularity,
    which allows users to tailor the resulting circuit to the gate sets best suited for the target hardware.
    For instance, superconducting quantum hardware often implements Pauli gates ($X$, $Y$, $Z$) virtually, offering zero-error and zero-latency execution;
    in this case, the compiler can be configured to prioritize Pauli gates.
    Conversely, trapped-ion platforms such as IonQ natively support arbitrary single-qubit rotations,
    such as $R_x(\phi)$, $R_y(\phi)$, and $R_z(\phi)$, and the compiler can be adapted accordingly to produce rotation-based circuits.
    This hardware-aware configurability enables efficient, optimized circuit generation across diverse quantum backends
    while maintaining traceability and cross-platform compatibility.


    \section{Results}\label{sec:results}
    Initial benchmarks demonstrate that the compiler generates correct and consistent quantum circuits across multiple backends.
    These results highlight the compiler’s potential to accelerate quantum software development, circuit optimization, and experimentation across diverse quantum computing platforms.

    \section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion-and-future-work}
    Initial benchmarks confirm the correctness and consistency of the output across supported platforms, including Qiskit and Cirq.
    Future work includes incorporating parallelization to improve compilation efficiency for higher-dimensional Hamiltonians.
    We also plan to extend compatibility to additional frameworks, such as AWS Braket and PennyLane,
    with the broader goal of streamlining quantum algorithm development across the heterogeneous quantum computing landscape~\cite{braket-zenodo,pennylane}.
    In addition, we aim to integrate quantum error correction capabilities to enhance the reliability of synthesized circuits.
    We invite collaboration and feedback from the quantum software community.
